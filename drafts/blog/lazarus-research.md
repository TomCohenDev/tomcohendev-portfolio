## Sources checked November 12, 2026

- [Lazarus paper (PDF)](https://github.com/anadim/anadim.github.io/blob/master/papers/Lazarus.pdf): the primary source for all numbers. Authors: Zeng, Severi, Bullwinkel, Horvitz, Papailiopoulos (Microsoft Research and the Microsoft AI Red Team). Key tables: Table 1 (23 samples), Table 2 (ASR and leakage), Table 3 (AIME and Terminal-Bench), Table 5 (LLM audits), Table 6 (feature detector), Table 14 (free generation, 0.14% leakage).
- [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazare-veni-foras-reprogramming-dormant-neurons-to-backdoor-ai-agents/), November 10: the "enough RAM" quote, the "no scan can prove" quote, the statement that this is not a case against open weights, and the Foundry controls.
- [Microsoft Threat Intelligence guidance](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/10/detecting-sleeper-agents-hiding-in-ai-model-neurons/), November 10: diff guidance ("broadly and diffusely" versus "a few weight vectors by a large amount"), hashes, private registry, sandboxing, egress controls, approvals, PyRIT. Reports no in-the-wild use.
- [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/microsoft-researchers-show-ai-agents.html), November 11. The page returns 403 to automated fetches, so Tom pasted the text. It is the source for the X walkthrough details (Node.js task, connection-error trigger, root in a test container) and the 51% to 99.5% range, which is consistent with paper Tables 1 and 2.
- [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/11/microsoft-hidden-ai-backdoors-lazarus/), November 11: consistent with the above.

## Editorial choices

- The attack section explains the mechanism conceptually: access, trigger and payload, dormant neurons, detector and writer, fitting cost, evasion, execution. It deliberately omits equations, the selection and fitting procedure, hyperparameters, and any code. It does not link the `microsoft/lazarus` code repository named in the paper.
- The defensive diff script was tested only on synthetic bf16 checkpoints. It flagged a seven-neuron last-layer edit with and without added noise, and did not flag a diffuse low-rank change. It has not been run against a real backdoored or real fine-tuned model, and the article says so.
- Discovery date: the paper says Claude Code with Opus 4.5 found the core idea in February 2026, while the Microsoft Research blog says "back in March." The article says "early 2026."
- The THN mention of an earlier ProjectDiscovery proof of concept (October 6, fine-tuning plus Codex CLI) was not independently verified, so it is left out.
- The "OpenClaw and Hermes Agent default" claim is attributed to the paper.
- Eight figures per language, of different types: an attack-surface map, a neuron anatomy cutaway, a token-by-step heat grid, a sequence diagram of the Node.js walkthrough, a results chart (paper Tables 2 and 3), an audit-outcome chart (Tables 5 and 6), a log-scale diff chart of the synthetic checkpoints, and a layered defense model. `assets/lazarus/generate.py` writes them to `public/blog/lazarus/`. Hebrew versions mirror the layout. Chart fills were validated for the dark surface with the dataviz palette checks. The diff chart reads `assets/lazarus/synthetic-row-norms.json`, produced by `synthetic_fixtures.py` and `synthetic_norms.py`. Activation shapes and the heat grid are illustrative, and the payload domain is a placeholder (`attacker.invalid`).
